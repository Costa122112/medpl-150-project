Reading Reflection: On Photography by Susan Sontag

Photography and documenting moments has become a big part of the way I remember experiences in my life. I know many people always say "live in the moment" but when I take pictures that is me living in the moment, it's just something I enjoy doing. I use photography in many different ways, from taking pictures with my phone to using a digital camera (Kodak fz 55, and cannon). Most of the time, I take pictures when traveling, trying new foods, going out with friends, or simply when I see something that I think looks interesting. I do not always take a picture with the intention of making something look perfect or aesthetic. Sometimes, its simply to document the moment and be able to look back at it later. However, I have realized that even these simple pictures can say a lot about who I am and the time that I live in.

Susan Sontag's In Plato's Cave made me think more about what it actually means to take a photograph. She discusses how cameras have become a major part of everyday life and how people use photographs to experience and remember the world. Even though she wrote this before smartphones and social media became as common as they are today, her ideas still apply. Today, almost everyone has a camera with them at all times whether it's a phone or a physical camera. We constantly photograph our food, friends, vacations, events, and even ourselves. Because of this, taking pictures can sometimes feel automatic which could be a good or bad thing depending on your mindset and opinion on photography

One of the main ways I use photography is through my phone. Since my phone is usually always with me, it allows me to capture moments almost instantly. I take pictures of food, sunsets, and just everyday experiences with my phone. I also use photography to share parts of my life with other people. I don't really post much on social media so when I'm sharing my pictures with people it's usually just family, friends, and co workers. However I do have a story highlight on instagram with just places ive traveled to.

I also have a couple digital cameras but the one ive been using most recently is a Kodak fz 55. I recently took this camera with me on a trip to Greece and Cyprus and I must say some of the pictures it took were breathtaking. Using my digital camera actually makes me pay much more attention to the settings I'm using as opposed to using an iphone which I feel like it just automatically fixes the settings for you depending on the lighting. For example, on my trip I constantly found myself having to change the settings depending on if it was day time or night time. One of the main settings I always found myself adjusting was the exposure. This has taught me that taking pictures isn't just about what's in front of you, but it's also about how you want that moment to be represented.

Therefore, taking pictures can mean a lot more than just simply clicking a button depending on the person. Personally it allows me to remember significant events that happened in my life while living in the moment. Reflecting on Sontag's arguments helped me see how capturing photos constantly in modern times has turned photography into an automatic habit of our daily routines. Photography can also tell us not only what the world looks like, but also what we value about the world around us.

Reading Reflection: Your Brain on Chat GPT

One thing that stood out to me from both the video and the article “Your Brain on ChatGPT” was the fact that AI can be extremely useful while also changing and effecting the way we think. AI is now part of a lot of people's everyday lives, especially for college students. While AI can help explain difficult concepts, generate ideas, organize information, and make completing certain tasks much faster it seems as if people are relying way too much on AI to just do simple thinking and work for them. Both the article and the video made me think more about what happens when we start relying on AI to do too much of the work and or thinking for us.

I found the MIT research pretty interesting because they compared essays written by students using a search engine, ChatGPT, or no external resources. They used electroencephalography (EEG) to determine levels of brain activity across the three groups and were able to distinguish between the groups. Students who wrote without the use of external resources displayed the highest level of brain connectivity, while students who used ChatGPT displayed the lowest overall level of connectivity. Also, students who used ChatGPT displayed the poorest performance in short-term recall and quoting of their own material. I found this interesting because it shows that there's a clear difference between completing an assignment and actually engaging with and learning from the assignment. This kind of reminds me of hand writing notes compared to typing notes on a laptop.

The video also expands on this big concern of AI and critical thinking. What I took away from it was not that AI itself is necessarily bad, but that our relationship with it matters and we need to be cautious of how we utilize it. Technology has always made certain tasks easier, and AI is another example of that. The problem begins when convenience turns into dependence. If someone immediately asks AI for an answer every time they encounter something difficult, they are removing some of the struggle involved in figuring things out themselves. That struggle can be frustrating, but it is also part of learning and developing problem-solving skills and I think that struggle is a necessity in our lives.

As a college student, this topic feels super relevant because AI is now almost impossible to ignore. I think there is a major difference between using AI as a tool and allowing it to replace your own thinking. For example, using it to clarify something you do not understand or to receive feedback on your own ideas can still require you to participate in the learning process. Having it completely produce your ideas and work for you is different because there is much less mental effort involved and this creates horrible habits and will only negatively impact your future.

Therefore, the article and video made me more aware of how easily convenience can affect the way we learn. At the same time, I do not think the answer is simply to stop using AI because let's face it, AI is now almost impossible to ignore because it is just everywhere. Instead, I think the more important question is how we can use AI without becoming dependent on it. AI can be a valuable tool, but we still need opportunities to struggle with ideas, think independently, and create things ourselves. Finding that balance will probably become even more important as AI continues to become a larger part of education and everyday life.

Reading Reflection: Motherhood in Crisis by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager and Saidu Bah

Motherhood is a subject that predominantly addresses child bearing, family maintenance, and the role of the parent. But motherhood can also involve some issues that are not as commonly acknowledged. Al Jazeera has released a publication on motherhood called Motherhood in Crisis. One of the stories featured in this publication is called “My Stolen Future.” This story features a young girl named Heela and highlights some of the issues that arise with teenage pregnancy in Sierra Leone. Heela’s story emphasizes the impact of teenage pregnancy on education, options, and the potential for the future. I found the use of images in addition to written text, video, and photographs to be very interesting. I believe that the use of multiple media types allowed for a personal yet informative experience.

I feel that pictures were used within this presentation in order for the audience to gain a better insight into Heela’s experiences. There are some experiences that cannot be effectively portrayed through photography but can be through the use of pictures. This can be significant when presenting experiences that may be awkward to photograph or experiences that occurred in the past. Pictures provide another method for the audience to gain insight into the experiences being presented aside from the text alone. I feel this aids the presentation, as the audience is able to gain a visual insight into the content being communicated rather than having to infer it all themselves.

There is also a difference between illustration and photographic images. Photographic images depict real people and settings and therefore assist in conveying the reality of the situations presented. Illustrations, however, offer more creative freedom, and the artist is able to convey situations in a different way. Illustrations are more artistic than photographs, which are more realistic. The distinction is significant because each has a different function. Photography can be used to document reality while illustrations can be used to convey the emotion and personal nature embedded within that reality. In my opinion, the combination of both allows for a more well-rounded presentation of the story, as both the reality and the personal aspects can be conveyed.

Another thing that was interesting was how illustrations can communicate emotions that might be difficult to express through photographs or written text alone. In Heela's story, teenage pregnancy is not the only focus, but also how it can change the direction of someone's life. Feelings such as fear, uncertainty, and disappointment may not always be easy to capture in a photograph. Illustrations give the creators more freedom to represent these emotions visually. This can help readers connect with the story on a deeper level and understand that the challenges Heela faces go beyond simply becoming a young mother.

Therefore, My Stolen Future is able to use different types of media in order to deliver a more complete message. Prior to reading this piece I had always thought of images as being used primarily for recreation and not in news pieces. This piece has demonstrated to me that images can also be effective in a piece about a real issue. I found in this piece that the manner in which information is conveyed can affect interpretation. Through the use of images, photographs, and text, the creators conveyed Heela’s story in a fashion that allows the issues facing young motherhood and the value of future opportunity to be understood.
